#### ch01-el05 · question · standard
- format: plainText
- question: Verses 1–2 speak about the Teacher (‘laments the Teacher’) rather than as the Teacher. What does that suggest about how the book is put together?
- linkedPassage: Ecclesiastes 1:1–11
- answerPlaceholder: Your thoughts…
- questionHint: Notice who is being described, and who is doing the describing.
- sampleAnswer: The Teacher is introduced in the third person, so someone other than the Teacher is presenting him and reporting his words. That suggests there are two voices in the book: the Teacher himself, and a narrator who introduces him to us. Later we shall see whether that second voice returns. For now, we are being told to listen to the Teacher while remembering that we are hearing him through someone else.
- keyTerms: ["someone else|another voice|narrator|third person|introduc", "teacher", "two voices|two speakers|frame"]
- mainPoint: The book has **two voices**: a narrator who introduces the Teacher, and the Teacher himself, whose words we are about to hear.
- mainPointFormat: markdown

#### ch01-el06 · question · standard
- format: plainText
- question: Verse 2 is the verdict that the rest of the book will test. How is it worded, and what shows how sweeping it is?
- linkedPassage: Ecclesiastes 1:1–11
- answerPlaceholder: Your thoughts…
- questionHint: Look at the repetition and at the last word of the verse.
- sampleAnswer: The verdict is that everything is futile, and it is said with heavy repetition: ‘Futile! Futile!’, then ‘Absolutely futile! Everything is futile!’. The word ‘absolutely’ and the word ‘everything’ leave no obvious exceptions. It is a bold claim, and the reader is invited to see whether the Teacher can sustain it.
- keyTerms: ["futile", "absolutely|repeat", "everything|all|no exception"]
- mainPoint: The Teacher opens with a **sweeping verdict**: everything is futile. The rest of the book tests whether he can sustain it.

#### ch01-el08 · question · standard
- format: plainText
- question: What question does the Teacher ask in verse 3, and what evidence do verses 4–7 offer?
- linkedPassage: Ecclesiastes 1:1–11
- answerPlaceholder: Your thoughts…
- questionHint: Look at what stays the same and what keeps moving.
- sampleAnswer: He asks what benefit people get from all the effort they expend on earth. The evidence is taken from nature: generations come and go while the earth remains, the sun rises and sets, the wind circles round, and the streams flow into a sea that is never full. Everything moves, but nothing arrives anywhere new.
- keyTerms: ["benefit|profit|gain", "generation", "sun|wind|stream|sea", "repeat|round|circle|same"]
- mainPoint: The Teacher is asking about **lasting benefit**, and nature’s endless cycles are his first evidence that human effort gains little that lasts.
- mainPointFormat: markdown

#### ch01-el09 · question · standard
- format: plainText
- question: What do verses 8–10 add to the picture, and what would the Teacher say to someone proud of a brand-new invention?
- linkedPassage: Ecclesiastes 1:1–11
- answerPlaceholder: Your thoughts…
- questionHint: Think about the eye, the ear, and the word ‘new’.
- sampleAnswer: The Teacher says that all this repetition is tiresome and beyond description, and that the eye and ear are never satisfied. He adds that nothing is truly new: what exists now is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done. To the proud inventor he would reply that the thing they call new has probably already been done long ago, before our time.

- mainPoint: Repetition, weariness and the lack of anything truly new leave **the eye and ear unsatisfied**.

He finds few direct promises or types of Christ in the book, and often finds that the New Testament affirms the Teacher’s call to enjoy God’s gifts while adding the resurrection hope that the Teacher lacks. Read closely for the Christ sections of every passage used. My New Testament pairings (Rom 8, Luke 12, 1 Cor 1, Gal 4, Acts 17, Luke 4, Gal 6, Heb 4, 1 Tim 6, John 11, 1 Cor 15, Col 2, John 10, 2 Cor 5) are drawn from the stock of connections he and others make; the questions on them are my own. He treats Solomon as a type of Christ and reads ‘one shepherd’ in 12:11 as pointing to the coming Davidic shepherd. I have not used the Solomon typology or his reading of the shepherd; following your direction I adapted the typology to the wise man in general.
- **Longman, *The Book of Ecclesiastes* (NICOT, Eerdmans, 1998).** Argues that a frame narrator introduces and closes the book and that the body is Qohelet’s own, sometimes unorthodox, voice; compares the structure with Job. Sees Qohelet as describing life ‘under the sun’ accurately but without hope of redemption, and sees the epilogue as the book’s corrective. Points to Romans 8:18–21 as the nearest New Testament echo. This matches your position and shaped session 1 (question 1), session 8 (question 4) and the Romans 8 pairing. Read in the sections on theology, the New Testament perspective and the epilogue.
- **Provan, *Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs* (NIV Application Commentary, Zondervan, 2001).** Doubts that Solomon wrote the book and sees the speaker as adopting a royal role. Argues that the key word means something breath-like and fleeting, not ‘meaningless’, and that one author can look at life from several angles. Divides the book passage by passage, as I have. Used for the how-to note on the key word and for the cautious handling of authorship.
- **Bartholomew, *Ecclesiastes* (Baker Academic, 2009).** A long literary and theological commentary that also reads the book with a frame narrator and gives a detailed outline (including ‘the mystery of time’, ‘four problems’, ‘worship’, ‘the fate of death and the gift of life’, ‘rejoicing and remembering’). Read lightly only: contents, front matter and the frame-narrator discussion. It supports the passage divisions rather than supplying any content.
- **Eaton, *Ecclesiastes* (Tyndale, IVP, 1983).** An older evangelical commentary. Sees an author-editor in Solomon’s tradition (not Solomon), reads the ‘one Shepherd’ of 12:11 as God, and lists ‘eternity’ as the commonest reading of 3:11. Read lightly, on authorship, 3:11 and 12:11 only.

### Points for your decision (theology and translation)
1. **Frame voice.** Handled as an observation about who speaks, not as a correction of the Teacher. Say if you want a stronger statement, for example in the session 8 closing.
2. **Authorship.** The study never calls the Teacher Solomon. Sessions refer to ‘the Teacher’. NET 1:1 says ‘son of David, king in Jerusalem’.
3. **‘Enjoy life’ refrain.** Presented as a real gift from God (2:24–26, 3:12–13, 5:18–20, 9:7–10). Session 7, question 6 says the joy is ‘under the shadow of death, with nothing said here about hope beyond the grave’. Check that this wording is how you want to put it.
4. **3:11.** NET reads ‘ignorance’ in the human heart; Provan, Eaton (as the commonest reading), Longman and Bartholomew read ‘eternity’. Session 3 has an inline note saying translations differ, and the question avoids the contested word. Keep or remove the note.
5. **3:18–21.** The question stays with what the text says (same fate, open question). The resurrection answer is given in Looking to Christ (Acts 17:30–31; John 11).
6. **2:26 (the sinner’s task).** Called ‘a hard verse’; the question only asks what is given to each group. Commentators differ on whether it is Qohelet’s own view or a questionable one.
7. **11:9.** Sample answer reads ‘God will judge your motives and actions’ as real judgment, in line with 12:14. Some read 11:9b as the frame voice’s addition; that fits your frame reading and needs no change.
8. **12:5–7.** The pictures are read “most naturally” as old age and death. Other readings (a storm, a house falling) exist.
9. **12:11, ‘one shepherd’.** Left open on purpose (Greidanus: the coming Davidic shepherd; Eaton: God). The Looking to Christ bridge says readers differ and does not decide.
10. **Wise man as a type of Christ.** Greidanus’s Solomon typology is not used. As you directed, the wise man in general is presented as a pointer to Christ, the wisdom from God (session 2 bridge and 1 Corinthians 1:22–31; session 8 bridge and Colossians 2:1–3). The bridges say the New Testament ‘presents Christ as the true wise man’ and the questions use ‘can be read as pointing beyond himself’, not a firm claim about what the Teacher intended. Check that the strength of the wording suits you.
11. **Session 6 choice.** I chose 5:10–20 (money and the gift of God) over 7:15–29 (wisdom and its limits) because it is easier for first-time readers and carries the ‘gift of God’ thread. Wisdom’s limits are covered in the catch-up summary of chapters 6–8 and in 9:11–12. Say if you would rather swap.
12. **Two or more New Testament passages in some sessions** (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8; session 8 has three). They are short; I can reduce to one each if sessions run long.
